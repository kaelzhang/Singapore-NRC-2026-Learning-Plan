# W03 Teacher Notes

## Aim
Students should see agriculture as a long chain of problem-solving tools and tradeoffs.

## Pacing
- 15 min: Quick timeline from wild food to modern farming.
- 25 min: Student reading and vocabulary.
- 25 min: Timeline with problem and tradeoff.
- 20 min: Convert one old farming tool into a robot mission idea.
- 10 min: Share one tradeoff.

## Misconceptions To Watch
- "Old farming was simple." It required deep knowledge of seasons, seeds, soil, water, and storage.
- "More yield is always better." Yield matters, but so do soil, water, cost, nutrition, and fairness.
- "Technology keeps improving in a straight line." Many tools solve one problem while creating another.

## NRC Link
This week builds the logic for production infrastructure. Students should start recognizing that a mission object can represent a deep historical problem, such as water control or labour reduction.

## Evidence Of Learning
Each student should explain one agricultural innovation using this sentence:

"This helped because..., but the tradeoff was..."
