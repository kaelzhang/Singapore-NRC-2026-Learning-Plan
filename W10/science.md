## Big Question
How does food move safely from where it is produced to where people eat it?

## Why This Week Matters
Food is not secure just because it was produced. It must be stored, cooled when needed, transported, tracked, delivered, and handled safely. Food logistics is the movement layer of Mission Meals.

In NRC terms, logistics becomes route planning, docking, loading, unloading, sorting, and safe delivery.

## The One-Minute Idea
Food logistics is the system that moves food. A cold chain keeps temperature-sensitive food cold from production to eating. Traceability records where food came from and where it went. Warehouses and robots can help organize movement, but safety and reliability matter more than speed alone.

## Past, Present, Future

### Past
Before refrigeration, people used drying, salting, smoking, fermenting, ice, cellars, and local markets to keep food usable. Long-distance fresh food was difficult.

### Present
Modern logistics uses refrigerated trucks, cold rooms, ports, warehouses, scanners, barcodes, sensors, delivery platforms, and automated storage systems. If cold chain breaks, food can spoil or become unsafe.

### Future
Future food logistics may use real-time temperature tracking, autonomous delivery vehicles, warehouse robots, blockchain or other traceability systems, predictive demand planning, and smarter stockpiles. The challenge is to make supply chains resilient, safe, and affordable.

## Deep Explanation

### 1. Logistics Is About Flow
A food system has many flows:

- physical food,
- temperature,
- time,
- money,
- information,
- safety records,
- packaging,
- waste.

If physical food moves but information is missing, a recall becomes harder. If information is good but the cold chain fails, food can still spoil.

### 2. Cold Chain Protects Temperature-Sensitive Food
Some foods need controlled temperature: seafood, meat, dairy, eggs, frozen food, vaccines, and some fresh produce. A cold chain is the linked system of cold storage and cold transport.

For an eight-year-old: cold chain is like keeping an ice cream cold during its whole journey, not just at the start and end.

### 3. Warehouses Are Decision Places
A warehouse is not just a big room. It decides where items go, which order leaves first, how to avoid damage, and how to keep records. Automated warehouses use robots and software to move bins, shelves, or packages.

### 4. Traceability Helps When Something Goes Wrong
Traceability means being able to follow food from source to destination. If a food safety problem happens, traceability helps identify which batch is affected and where it went.

### 5. Speed Is Not The Only Goal
Fast delivery is useful, but not if food gets crushed, warmed, lost, or mixed up. A logistics robot must move accurately, dock reliably, and release objects gently.

## Core Terms

### Logistics
Planning and moving goods, information, and resources from one place to another.

### Cold Chain
A temperature-controlled supply chain for products that must stay cold.

### Warehouse
A place where goods are stored, sorted, tracked, and prepared for movement.

### Traceability
The ability to track where food came from and where it went.

### Batch
A group of products made or handled together.

### Docking
Moving into a precise position for loading, unloading, or charging.

### Inventory
The goods currently stored and tracked.

### Recall
Removing unsafe or problematic products from sale or use.
