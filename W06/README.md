# W06 - Controlled Environment Agriculture, Hydroponics, And Vertical Farms

## Big Question
Can people grow food indoors, in cities, and even in stacked layers?

## Why This Week Matters
Singapore has limited land. Controlled Environment Agriculture, or CEA, is one way to grow selected crops in smaller spaces by controlling light, water, temperature, humidity, and nutrients.

This week connects plant science to urban farming technology.

## The One-Minute Idea
Controlled Environment Agriculture means growing plants in a managed space such as a greenhouse, indoor farm, or plant factory. Vertical farming stacks growing layers upward. Hydroponics grows plants without soil. These systems can save land and sometimes water, but they may need electricity, skilled operators, and careful monitoring.

## Past, Present, Future
### Past
People have protected plants for a long time using walls, glasshouses, shade, and irrigation. The goal was to reduce the risk from bad weather and extend growing seasons.

### Present
Modern CEA uses sensors, climate control, LED lighting, nutrient dosing, pumps, cameras, and software. In Singapore, CEA and urban farms are important because they can produce selected fresh foods close to consumers.

### Future
Future CEA may use smarter energy systems, better LED recipes, lower-cost automation, recycled water, plant-growth AI, and building-integrated farms. The biggest question may be energy: can indoor farms be productive without using too much electricity?

## Deep Explanation
### 1. CEA Tries To Move "Weather" Indoors
Outdoor farms depend on sun, rain, wind, soil, and seasons. CEA tries to control some of these variables. Instead of waiting for the right weather, a farm can set light time, temperature, humidity, and water flow.

For an eight-year-old: CEA is like giving plants a room where the weather is managed for them.

### 2. Vertical Farming Uses Space Upward
If land is scarce, one idea is to stack plant trays in layers. This increases production per floor area. But stacked plants need artificial light or careful light delivery. They also need airflow so heat and humidity do not build up.

### 3. Hydroponics And Aeroponics Are Root Systems
Hydroponics gives roots nutrient water. Aeroponics mists roots with nutrient solution. Aquaponics combines fish and plants in a linked system. These systems are different, but all require careful balance.

### 4. Automation Helps Repetition And Monitoring
Indoor farms repeat many tasks: moving trays, checking sensors, dosing nutrients, cleaning systems, harvesting, and packing. Robots and automation can help if the task is clear and frequent.

### 5. The Tradeoff Is Control Versus Cost
More control can reduce some risks, but it can also increase cost and complexity. A vertical farm may save land and reduce pesticide use, but it may need electricity for lights and cooling.

## Core Terms
### Controlled Environment Agriculture
Growing plants in a managed environment where conditions such as light, temperature, humidity, and nutrients are controlled.

### Greenhouse
A structure that protects plants while allowing light in and climate control.

### Plant Factory
An indoor growing facility that uses high control, often with artificial lighting and stacked layers.

### Vertical Farming
Growing crops in stacked layers to use vertical space.

### Aeroponics
Growing plants with roots hanging in air and misted with nutrient solution.

### Aquaponics
A system that links fish farming and plant growing, using fish waste nutrients for plants.

### Energy Efficiency
How much useful output is gained from each unit of energy.

### Climate Control
Managing temperature, humidity, airflow, and sometimes carbon dioxide.

## Robot Connection
CEA is a natural robotics environment because it has structured rows, trays, markers, and repeated tasks.

Possible robot tasks:

- visit each growing rack,
- move seedling trays,
- deliver a nutrient token,
- sort harvested vegetables,
- check colour markers,
- carry a crate from growth area to packing area.

NRC translation:

```text
Stacked farm -> narrow robot paths
Sensor station -> robot stops at markers
Tray movement -> robot uses a pusher or lifter
```
