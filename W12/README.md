# W12 - Climate Risk, Extreme Heat, Water Stress, And Resilient Agriculture

## Big Question
How can food systems keep working when heat, drought, floods, and weather shocks become stronger?

## Why This Week Matters
Food systems depend on climate. Crops, livestock, fish, workers, storage, and transport can all be affected by extreme weather. A Mission Meals robot should be designed with resilience in mind.

This week connects climate science to agriculture and engineering.

## The One-Minute Idea
Climate risk means the chance that climate conditions will harm a system. Extreme heat can reduce crop yields, stress animals, make outdoor work dangerous, warm water bodies, and increase storage problems. Resilient agriculture tries to prepare, adapt, and recover.

For an eight-year-old: resilience means a system can get bumped and still keep going.

## Past, Present, Future
### Past
Farmers have always dealt with weather risk. They used planting calendars, seed saving, irrigation, terraces, shade, crop diversity, and food storage to survive uncertain seasons.

### Present
FAO and WMO warn that extreme heat is a major and growing threat to agrifood systems. Heat affects crops, livestock, aquaculture, forests, and workers. Climate shocks can also combine with conflict, prices, pests, and supply-chain problems.

### Future
Future resilience may use heat-tolerant crops, improved irrigation, shade systems, early-warning services, climate data, protected cultivation, crop diversity, cooler logistics, and emergency stockpiles. But adaptation has limits if warming continues to increase.

## Deep Explanation
### 1. Heat Affects Biology
Plants have temperature ranges where they grow well. Too much heat can reduce photosynthesis, damage flowers, dry soil, and increase water stress. Animals can suffer heat stress, eat less, grow more slowly, or become ill.

### 2. Water Stress Is More Than "No Water"
Water stress can mean:

- not enough rain,
- water at the wrong time,
- irrigation water too expensive,
- salty water,
- floods that damage roots,
- competition between farms, cities, and ecosystems.

### 3. Climate Risk Is A Chain
A heat wave can reduce crop yield. Lower yield can raise prices. Higher prices can reduce access to healthy food. Heat can also affect workers, transport, and cold storage. One climate event can travel through the whole food system.

### 4. Early Warning Helps People Act Earlier
Weather forecasts, heat alerts, pest monitoring, and water data can give people time to protect crops, adjust work hours, move stock, irrigate, or prepare supplies.

### 5. Resilience Uses Diversity And Backup
A resilient system usually does not rely on only one crop, one supplier, one route, one storage method, or one technology. Diversity and backup give options when a shock happens.

## Core Terms
### Climate Risk
The chance that climate conditions will harm people, ecosystems, or systems.

### Extreme Heat
Very high temperatures that can harm health, crops, animals, or infrastructure.

### Heat Stress
Harmful stress on plants, animals, or people caused by high temperature.

### Water Stress
When water supply, quality, timing, or access is not enough for needs.

### Adaptation
Changing systems or behaviour to reduce harm from climate impacts.

### Mitigation
Reducing greenhouse gas emissions or increasing carbon storage to slow climate change.

### Early-Warning System
A system that uses data and forecasts to warn people before danger becomes worse.

### Resilience
The ability to prepare for, survive, and recover from shocks.

## Robot Connection
Climate resilience missions can become:

```text
heat alert -> robot moves shade marker
water stress -> robot delivers water token
storm warning -> robot moves food to safe storage
cold chain risk -> robot checks temperature station
```

Robots can also gather data after a shock:

- inspect damage,
- check sensors,
- move emergency supplies,
- sort safe and unsafe items.
