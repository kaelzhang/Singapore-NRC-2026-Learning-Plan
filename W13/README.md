# W13 - Future Foods: Alternative Proteins, Fermentation, Cultured Meat, Insects, And Algae

## Big Question
If traditional food production faces land, water, climate, and animal-disease limits, what new food sources might help?

## Why This Week Matters
Mission Meals is about future food systems, not only today's farms. Future foods include plant-based meat, precision fermentation, biomass fermentation, cultivated meat, insects, algae, and other protein sources.

This topic needs careful thinking. New foods are not automatically good or bad. We must ask about safety, nutrition, cost, energy, taste, culture, regulation, and scale.

## The One-Minute Idea
Alternative proteins are protein sources that do not rely only on conventional livestock. Some come from plants. Some come from microbes. Some come from animal cells grown in controlled conditions. Some come from insects or algae. Each has different benefits, risks, and questions.

For an eight-year-old: future food is not one strange thing. It is a toolbox of many possible ways to make protein.

## Past, Present, Future
### Past
Humans have long used alternative protein ideas without calling them that. Soy foods, beans, lentils, mushrooms, seaweed, and fermented foods are old. Many cultures have also eaten insects. Fermentation has been used for bread, yoghurt, soy sauce, tempeh, cheese, and many other foods.

### Present
Today, companies and researchers are developing plant-based meats, fermented proteins, mycoprotein, insect protein, algae-based foods, and cultivated meat. Singapore treats some of these as novel foods when they do not have a long history of safe consumption.

### Future
Future food systems may combine conventional agriculture with alternative proteins. The strongest future may not be "replace everything." It may be a mixed system that uses each source where it makes sense.

## Deep Explanation
### 1. Protein Is A Nutrient, Not A Food Category
Protein helps build and repair body tissues. It is found in meat, fish, eggs, dairy, beans, lentils, soy, nuts, seeds, grains, fungi, algae, and insects.

The food system question is not only "where can protein come from?" It is also "can people safely, affordably, and willingly eat it?"

### 2. Plant-Based Meat Uses Plant Ingredients
Plant-based meat tries to imitate some properties of animal meat using plant proteins, fats, flavours, colours, and texture design. It may use soy, pea, wheat, or other ingredients.

Tradeoff: it can reduce reliance on animals, but it may still be processed and must be judged by nutrition, cost, taste, and environmental evidence.

### 3. Fermentation Uses Microorganisms
Fermentation can use yeasts, bacteria, or fungi. In biomass fermentation, the microbe itself becomes food. In precision fermentation, a microbe is programmed or selected to produce a specific ingredient, such as a protein.

For an eight-year-old: microbes can be tiny food factories.

### 4. Cultivated Meat Grows Animal Cells
Cultivated meat is grown from animal cells in controlled equipment such as bioreactors. The cells need nutrients, safe conditions, and careful process control.

This is scientifically exciting, but scale and cost are difficult. It also requires strong safety assessment and clear labelling.

### 5. Insects And Algae Are Real Food Sources
Insects can be rich in protein and may use less land than some conventional systems. Algae can grow in water systems and may provide proteins, oils, pigments, or other nutrients. But culture, taste, safety, processing, and regulation all matter.

## Core Terms
### Alternative Protein
Protein from sources other than conventional livestock or seafood systems.

### Plant-Based Meat
A food designed to resemble meat but made mainly from plant ingredients.

### Fermentation
Using microorganisms to transform food or produce useful substances.

### Precision Fermentation
Using microbes to produce a specific ingredient, often a protein or flavour molecule.

### Biomass Fermentation
Growing microorganisms so the microbial biomass itself becomes food.

### Cultivated Meat
Meat grown from animal cells in controlled conditions rather than by raising and slaughtering an animal.

### Bioreactor
Controlled equipment where cells or microbes grow.

### Novel Food
A food without a long history of safe consumption, requiring safety assessment before sale in places such as Singapore.

## Robot Connection
Future foods still need robots and automation:

- move trays or bioreactor samples,
- monitor temperature and contamination risk,
- sort ingredients,
- package products,
- track batches,
- clean equipment,
- deliver products through cold chain.

NRC translation:

```text
bioreactor process -> precise station sequence
new protein product -> sorting and labelling
food safety -> traceable batch movement
```
