## Big Question
What does a plant need to grow food, and why do farmers care so much about light, water, and nutrients?

## Why This Week Matters
Food Production Infrastructure missions are about helping food grow. To understand those missions, you need to understand the living system inside a plant.

A robot that checks a farm, moves a plant tray, turns on a light, or delivers water is supporting a biological process. The robot is not growing the plant by magic. It is helping control the conditions that let the plant grow.

## The One-Minute Idea
Plants make sugar through photosynthesis. They need light energy, carbon dioxide, water, and nutrients. They also need the right temperature, enough space, and protection from stress. Different farming systems control these needs in different ways.

For an eight-year-old: a plant is like a tiny food factory. Light is energy, water is a material, air gives carbon dioxide, and nutrients are building blocks.

## Past, Present, Future

### Past
Early farmers learned from observation. They noticed that crops grew better in certain soils, seasons, and watering patterns. They did not know the chemistry of photosynthesis, but they knew that sun, water, and soil mattered.

### Present
Today, scientists can measure light intensity, light spectrum, soil moisture, nutrient levels, pH, temperature, humidity, and plant stress. Farmers can use soil tests, hydroponic nutrient solutions, greenhouse sensors, and LED lights to control plant growth more precisely.

### Future
Future plant production may use smarter lighting recipes, water recycling, plant stress sensors, AI crop models, climate-resilient varieties, and robots that inspect individual plants. The challenge is to grow more nutritious food while using less land, water, energy, and chemicals.

## Deep Explanation

### 1. Photosynthesis Is The Plant's Food-Making Process
Photosynthesis happens mainly in leaves. A green pigment called chlorophyll captures light energy. The plant uses that energy to combine carbon dioxide from air and water from roots into sugars.

The simple idea is:

```text
light + carbon dioxide + water -> sugar + oxygen
```

The sugar becomes energy and building material for the plant. It helps make stems, roots, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds.

### 2. Light Has Different Colours And Energy
Light is made of different wavelengths. Plants use some wavelengths more strongly than others. Red and blue light are important for photosynthesis, but plants also respond to other colours for shape, timing, and stress signals.

This is why vertical farms and greenhouses may use LED lights. LEDs can be tuned so plants receive useful light. But lights need electricity, so energy cost is a serious tradeoff.

### 3. Water Moves Nutrients
Water does not only stop plants from drying out. It also carries dissolved nutrients from roots into the plant. Plants lose water through tiny openings in leaves in a process called transpiration. This helps move water upward, but it also means hot weather can stress plants.

### 4. Soil Is More Than Dirt
Soil can hold water, air, minerals, organic matter, microbes, and roots. Healthy soil is a living system. Some modern farms grow plants without soil, but they must still provide water, nutrients, oxygen, and root support.

### 5. Hydroponics Replaces Soil With A Nutrient Solution
Hydroponics means growing plants with roots in water or another support material while nutrients are dissolved in the water. It can save space and water, and it can be used indoors. But it needs careful monitoring. If pumps fail or nutrient balance is wrong, plants can suffer quickly.

## Core Terms

### Photosynthesis
The process plants use to turn light energy, carbon dioxide, and water into sugar.

### Chlorophyll
The green pigment that helps plants capture light energy.

### Spectrum
The range of colours or wavelengths in light.

### Photon
A tiny packet of light energy.

### Nutrient
A substance a plant needs to grow, such as nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, or magnesium.

### Transpiration
The movement of water through a plant and out through leaves as water vapour.

### Hydroponics
Growing plants without soil by giving roots water with dissolved nutrients.

### pH
A measure of how acidic or alkaline a liquid is. Nutrient solutions need the right pH so roots can take up nutrients.
