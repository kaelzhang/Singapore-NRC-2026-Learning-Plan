# W04 Science Brief - Food Security, Hunger, Nutrition, And Affordability

## Big Idea
The world can produce a lot of food, but many people still cannot get enough safe, nutritious, affordable food. This is why **Food Security** is more than harvest size. It also includes prices, income, conflict, climate, transport, safety, health, culture, and waste.

A strong food system must help people eat well, not only produce more tonnes of food.

## Past, Present, And Future
In the past, famines often involved crop failure, drought, war, weak storage, blocked transport, unfair rules, or poverty. Weather mattered, but human decisions also mattered. Food might exist somewhere else, but people could still starve if they could not reach or afford it.

Today, hunger and food insecurity remain global problems. Recent UN food-security reports estimate hundreds of millions of people facing hunger and billions facing moderate or severe food insecurity. Food prices, conflict, climate shocks, disease, debt, job loss, logistics problems, and unequal income all make access harder.

In the future, food security will need better production, stronger logistics, lower waste, safer food, more affordable healthy diets, climate adaptation, fair access, and early-warning systems. Technology can help, but fairness, trust, and public systems also matter.

## Food Security Has Four Dimensions
**Availability** means enough food exists. Farms, fisheries, imports, storage, and stockpiles all affect it.

**Access** means people can get the food. Money, prices, transport, shops, school meals, aid, and social support affect access.

**Utilization** means food is safe and nutritious, and people's bodies can use it. Clean water, cooking, health, food safety, and balanced diets matter.

**Stability** means availability, access, and utilization keep working over time. A family may eat well today but struggle next month if prices rise or a parent loses work.

Food security is weak if any one dimension fails.

## Hunger, Food Insecurity, And Malnutrition
**Hunger** often means not getting enough food energy. **Food insecurity** means people are unsure they can get enough safe and nutritious food. It can be mild, moderate, or severe.

**Malnutrition** means poor nutrition. It includes undernutrition, hidden hunger from missing vitamins or minerals, and overnutrition that can lead to unhealthy weight. A person can get enough calories but still lack iron, iodine, vitamin A, or other nutrients.

This is why a healthy diet matters. Rice or bread can provide energy, but people also need protein, vegetables, fruits, fats, fibre, vitamins, and minerals.

## Affordability Is A Real Food Problem
Food can be available in a country but still unaffordable for many families. **Affordability** depends on both prices and income. A healthy diet may cost more than a family can safely spend.

When prices rise, families may buy less food, choose cheaper but less nutritious food, skip meals, or reduce other needs. This affects children, health, learning, and long-term opportunity.

A robot cannot solve prices by itself, but a Mission Meals project can model access: emergency delivery, food rescue, school meals, stockpiles, or lower-waste logistics.

## Productivity Helps, But Is Not Enough
Higher farm productivity can improve availability and sometimes lower cost. But production alone cannot solve food insecurity if conflict blocks roads, poverty blocks access, food is unsafe, diets are unhealthy, or waste is high.

A good food-security explanation avoids the weak claim "just grow more food." A better claim is: "production, access, nutrition, safety, stability, and waste reduction must work together."

## Shocks Can Spread Through The System
A climate shock can reduce harvest. A lower harvest can raise prices. Higher prices can reduce access. A transport delay can spoil perishable food. Conflict can stop farming, block ports, and make food aid dangerous. A disease outbreak can affect workers, livestock, or trade.

This is why food security is a system. One shock can travel from farms to markets to families.

## Data And Early Warning
Early-warning systems use weather data, crop maps, prices, conflict information, and nutrition data to warn people before a crisis grows. But warnings only help if action follows: food aid, cash support, water help, seed support, school meals, or route planning.

Data must change action. This idea will return in precision agriculture and robot programming.

## Mission Meals Connections
A robot can model food-security dimensions. It can deliver food to a community zone, sort safe food from unsafe food, move a stockpile, choose a backup route, rescue surplus, or send stressed crops to inspection.

The explanation should name the dimension. For example, a cold-chain mission protects utilization because it helps keep food safe. A backup route supports stability. A food rescue mission supports access and waste reduction.

## Extra Details To Remember
Malnutrition has several forms. Undernutrition means the body does not get enough energy or nutrients. Hidden hunger means a person may eat enough calories but still miss important vitamins or minerals. Overnutrition can happen when diets contain too much energy and too little balance. All three can exist in the same country.

Healthy diets are a system problem. A family needs income, shops, time, cooking tools, clean water, safe food, and knowledge. A child may need school meals. A patient may need special nutrition. A city may need markets that sell fresh food near where people live.

Conflict is one of the strongest food-security shocks. It can stop farming, damage roads, block ports, raise prices, move families away from homes, and make aid dangerous. Food may exist in the world, but access breaks.

Climate shocks also hit more than crops. Heat can affect farm workers, animals, cold rooms, electricity demand, and transport timing. Drought can affect grain harvest and animal feed, which can change meat, eggs, dairy, and fish costs. Floods can damage food safety as well as yield.

Food waste and hunger can exist at the same time because the problem is not only total food amount. Safe surplus must be collected, stored, transported, matched to people, and used quickly. If the rescue system is missing, edible food can become waste while other people remain hungry.

A food-security claim should be specific. "Our robot helps food security" is too broad. "Our robot models a backup delivery route that supports stable access during a disruption" is stronger. It names the dimension and the action.

When reading numbers about hunger or food insecurity, remember that each number represents people. Data helps us see scale, but the goal is human well-being.

## Review Example: Same Food, Different Problem
A city may have enough rice in storage, but some families may still struggle because the price is too high. That is an access problem. A school may serve enough calories, but the meal may lack vegetables or micronutrients. That is a nutrition and utilization problem. A village may have fresh food today, but a flood may block the road next week. That is a stability problem.

These examples show why "more food" is not always the full answer. Food must also reach people, stay safe, support health, and keep coming during shocks.

For Mission Meals, try to name the exact problem. Is the robot helping availability, access, utilization, or stability? A clear dimension makes the mission easier to explain.
A good food-security mission also names who is helped: families, schools, shops, farms, or people facing emergency needs.
Do not forget time. A family may be secure in one season and insecure after job loss, price rise, illness, or transport disruption.

## Key Takeaways
Food security means more than enough food existing somewhere. People need food that is available, accessible, safe, nutritious, and stable over time. Hunger, malnutrition, affordability, conflict, climate, and logistics are connected.

Final check: pick one food-security problem and name the dimension it affects most. Then design one small robot action that models a helpful response.
