## Big Question
If the world produces so much food, why do many people still face hunger or poor nutrition?

## Why This Week Matters
Mission Meals is connected to SDG 2: Zero Hunger. But zero hunger is not only about full stomachs. It is also about safe food, healthy diets, fair access, stable supply, and farming that can continue into the future.

This week turns the big global problem into clear pieces you can reason about.

## The One-Minute Idea
Food security has four dimensions: availability, access, utilization, and stability. A place may have enough food in total but still have hunger if people cannot afford food, if supply is interrupted, if food is unsafe, or if diets are not nutritious.

Robotics can help production and logistics, but robots must fit inside this bigger human system.

## Past, Present, Future

### Past
In the past, famine was often caused by local crop failure, war, poor storage, or weak transport. If food could not move from a place with surplus to a place with shortage, people suffered.

### Present
SOFI 2025 estimates that about 673 million people experienced hunger in 2024. It also reports that 2.3 billion people experienced moderate or severe food insecurity. Food price inflation, conflict, climate shocks, poverty, and unequal access all affect whether people can eat healthy diets.

The modern world can move food across oceans, but this also means supply chains are complex. A disruption in grain, fuel, fertilizer, shipping, disease control, or finance can affect food prices far away.

### Future
Future food security will depend on resilient production, smarter logistics, better nutrition, fairer access, lower waste, climate adaptation, and stronger early-warning systems. Technology will help, but policy, community action, and affordability will matter too.

## Deep Explanation

### 1. Hunger Is Not Only A Farm Problem
If there is enough rice in a country but a family cannot afford it, the family still has an access problem.

If a child eats enough calories but very few vitamins, protein, or minerals, the child may still face malnutrition.

If food is available most months but disappears during storms, conflict, or price spikes, the system has a stability problem.

### 2. Nutrition Matters
Nutrition means the body gets the materials it needs to grow, repair, move, think, and stay healthy. A healthy diet needs more than energy. It needs protein, fats, carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals, fibre, and safe water.

For an eight-year-old: your body is like a building project. Calories are energy, but you also need many kinds of "building blocks."

### 3. Affordability Can Block Healthy Diets
Some foods are cheap and filling but not very diverse. Healthy diets can become too expensive when prices rise. That is why food security includes economics.

Robots might reduce some costs in farming or logistics, but only if the technology is affordable, repairable, and useful at the right scale.

### 4. Productivity Is Important But Not Enough
Productivity means producing more output from a given amount of land, water, labour, energy, or money. Higher productivity can help availability. But if higher productivity damages soil, uses too much water, or benefits only a few people, the system may not become truly secure.

## Core Terms

### Hunger
A condition where people do not get enough food energy over time.

### Food Insecurity
Uncertain or limited access to enough safe and nutritious food.

### Malnutrition
Poor nutrition. It can mean too little food, too little of important nutrients, or too much unhealthy food.

### Healthy Diet
A pattern of eating that gives the body enough energy and nutrients without causing harm.

### Affordability
Whether people can pay for what they need.

### Productivity
How much useful output a system produces from its inputs.

### Supply Shock
A sudden event that interrupts supply, such as drought, war, disease, export restrictions, or transport failure.

### Early-Warning System
A system that watches data so people can act before a food problem becomes a crisis.
